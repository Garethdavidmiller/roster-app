Getting Started

Open the app and the first thing you see is the sign-in card. Pick your grade, then your name, then type your password — your surname in lowercase, until you choose your own (the app asks you to). You stay signed in for up to 60 days.

No password yet, or just borrowing a station PC? Tap “Use the staff PIN instead” under the card. The PIN opens the roster, the Latest documents and 🚆 Trains until you close the tab. It doesn’t open Admin, Pay or Settings — they ask you to sign in as yourself, and opening one ends the PIN. On a shared PC, tap ☰ → End staff PIN when you finish.

Can’t get in? The card’s “Ask the admin to reset your password” link sends the request for you.

Once you are in, your name is already selected at the top and your roster appears. It works straight away in a browser — there is no app store to go to.

The key under the calendar shows AL, Absent, Spare, RDW, Other, Easter and Christmas only in a month that has them.

Christmas leave. From 2026, if your pattern has you on a shift on 25 or 26 December, the calendar shows that day as 🏖️ AL — unless it is a Sunday, which is never contracted. Neither day can be a spare day, so in a spare week it is a rest day. There is nothing to book, and it does not come off your allowance. Work 26 December when you were due to, and one day of leave in lieu is added to the next year’s allowance, to take at any time that year.

Tap a Day

Tapping or clicking any day opens a panel that answers four things in order:

  1. The date, and which roster week it falls in
  2. What the day is, written out in full — “Rest day”, “Annual leave”, or the shift times
  3. Whether anything has changed — ✓ As rostered if it still matches your pattern, or what was recorded instead
  4. What else the date carries — a 💷 Payday or ✂️ cut-off chip — and a button where one applies: Pay estimate on a pay day, Leave dates on an annual-leave day
On a computer, hovering over a day shows a quick preview; clicking opens the same panel. To go to the Pay Calculator from a pay day, use the panel’s Pay estimate button.

Works Offline

Your base roster is saved on your device after the first load, so the calendar always opens — even with no signal. Changes recorded in Admin, and the documents, need internet to load the latest.

The Menu

The ☰ button top-left opens the menu, which has three parts:

📄 Pages — Calendar, Admin, Pay, Trains (old and new timetables), Settings and any others you have access to
📋 Latest — Daily Huddle, Weekly Retail Circular, Marylebone Newsletter (for six weeks after each issue)
🔎 Reference — every guide, a search box and 📣 App Notices
Search the guides. Type “boxing day” in the box under Reference and it lists every guide section that mentions it.

At the bottom of the menu: your name, a 🔔 bell where your phone allows notifications, and Sign out — or, with the PIN, End staff PIN.

Trains

☰ → 🚆 Trains sets Chiltern’s timetable before its December 2026 change beside the one after it, as Marylebone sees them — for the customer who asks about “the 17:15”. Choose Weekdays, Saturdays or Sundays, then one of three views:

🔔 What’s changing — the biggest changes, one line per station, and Look up a time: type one and it says what happens to that train
🚉 Stations — one station, going there or coming back: the answer in a sentence, then the figures and every train that changes
🕰️ Where they stop — a daytime hour as a grid of which train stops where

The page turns with the date: from the change it says what changed, and the lookup answers from the new timetable. The staff PIN opens it too. Every card’s ? explains its words. These are published timetables, not live running — for tonight’s trains, use the live departure boards linked from its About panel.

Your Account

☰ → ⚙️ Settings is a checklist, not a form. It opens with a short “things to finish” summary, and each card carries its own status — Not saved, Using surname, Blocked — so you can see at a glance what is left.

🔑 Set your own password. Enter your current one, then a new one of at least 8 characters, not your surname, twice. Until then it is your surname — anyone who knows your name could guess it.
📧 Work email. Type just the firstname.surname part — the @chilternrailways.co.uk is already there. It is for account recovery. Self-service recovery isn't available yet: for now, ask the admin if you forget your password. Only you and the admin can see this email, and it doesn't change how you sign in.

Signing In

One sign-in covers every page. Signed in on the calendar? Tap 📝 Admin and it opens straight away.

Admin asks you to sign in only if you haven’t — for example, if you opened the roster with the staff PIN. Admin keeps your leave and changes under your own name, so you sign in as yourself.

  1. Pick your grade, then your name
  2. Enter your password — your surname by default (lowercase, no spaces or punctuation), or a password you've set yourself
  3. Tap Sign in — you stay signed in for up to 60 days on that phone or computer. On an iPhone, a Safari tab you haven’t opened for a week or so signs you out; the app on your Home Screen keeps you signed in
Example: "D. Smith" → the default password is smith
On a shared screen? Sign out from the menu (☰, at the bottom) when you finish.
Set your own password in ☰ → ⚙️ Settings → 🔑 Password — it replaces the surname default with one only you know. Until you do, the app asks you to choose one each time you sign in. Forgotten it? Speak to the admin, or use the sign-in card’s link to ask for a reset.

You can record leave and absence for yourself. Managers and the admin can also record changes for other staff; each card’s Recording for line names whose record it is.

Every Card Explains Itself

Each card has a ? button in its corner with the full steps for that card, written out. If something on this page is unclear, that is the quickest place to look — it is beside the thing it describes, and it is never out of date.

Change a Shift

  1. Check the name in the bar at the top — it is set to you. Managers and the admin can pick someone else there
  2. Move to the week you need — swipe or use ‹ ›, or tap the date label to open a calendar and jump
  3. Tick the day you want to change — its type buttons appear
  4. Tap a type: AL · Shift · RDW · Absent · Rest Day · Other
  5. Enter Start / End times for types that need them (24-hour, e.g. 06:20)
  6. Read the “You're about to…” line — it names the member and counts both what will change and what will be removed
  7. Tap Save changes
Check the receipt. After saving, the message lists one line per day, in date order, saying what each one now is — including days it removed. A count can't tell you whether you changed the days you meant to; the list can.
Already recorded? A day that already has a saved change opens with a ✓ Saved badge. Change it and it reads ⚠ Updating; select its type again and it reads ⚠ Removing — nothing is written until you Save.

Apply to Several Days

Above the day list is Apply to multiple days at once, in three numbered steps:

  1. Tick days — by hand, or with Mon–Fri, Working days or All 7
  2. Choose type — AL · Shift · RDW · Absent · Rest Day
  3. Apply to ticked days

It still only stages the change — you review the summary and press Save changes as usual. For leave spanning several days, the Record Annual Leave card and its date range is the easier route.

Leave & Absence

The 🏖️ Record Annual Leave and 🪑 Record Absence cards each take a date range. Neither uses a Sunday, which no grade is contracted for. An absence skips rest days automatically. For a CEA or CES, a whole Spare week off uses 4 days of leave, not 6.

Leave on a rest day is asked about, not assumed. A day the base roster calls rest normally costs no leave, and only the person booking knows whether the member had swapped a working day onto it — so the card asks, day by day, and will not save until every one is answered. Answer Rest day — free and nothing is recorded for that day; answer Swapped — counts and it is booked and uses a leave day. The leave card also shows the leave left in its header, for the year you are looking at (for example 2026: 12 left), so you can see the balance without opening anything.

Saved Changes

The 📋 Saved Changes card lists everything recorded — edit or remove any row. Filter by staff member and month. Rows tagged PDF upload came from the weekly roster PDF; you can still edit or remove them.

Sundays

Sundays are not contracted for any grade, so a Sunday offers only RDW, Rest Day and Spare (under Other) — no leave, absence or Shift. A worked Sunday is always RDW (1.5×; 3× on 26 December).

The "Other" Family

Tap Other and a small menu appears — pick Training, Induction, Assessment, Team Day, Union or Meeting. These pay as the day underneath (the shift they replace), so times are optional. On a rest day Rest day (RDW) is ticked and locked for you. On a working day, tick it only if the day was worked as a rest day — it then pays as RDW, not as the rostered shift.

📋 Spare also lives in the Other menu. It's a rarely-used placeholder for a standby day whose shift isn't set yet — it keeps its own purple badge and doesn't pay a premium.

Weekly Roster Upload Admins only

Admins upload the weekly roster PDF in Operations — an admin-only page most staff won't see. The app reads it and shows a review grouped by person: ticked rows will save, and where a day clashes with something already recorded it asks Keep saved or Use new roster. Nothing is written until you confirm, and the PDF itself is on the review to check against.

Rows can arrive unticked, and that is the app being careful. If somebody's week lines up better with their own pattern shifted a day, their rows start unticked with a warning — check each day against the PDF and tick only what you've confirmed. If several people drift the same way it isn't a coincidence, it's a misread: the whole upload is refused, nothing is selected and there is no Save button. Read the roster again rather than looking for a way round it.
A cell the app couldn't read gets a row you can answer in place — tap a type, and add times for Shift or RDW. Leave it alone and nothing is written for that day.
