1. What it does
 
• Your roster — shifts, rest days and annual leave, month by month. Swipe between months, tap a day for the detail.
• Record annual leave, or an absence.
• Team Week View — who is on with you.
• The Daily Huddle, Weekly Retail Circular and Marylebone Newsletter (for six weeks after each issue), plus guides for railcards, ranger and rover tickets, and FIP travel. All under the ☰ menu, top left.
2. Put it on your phone
 

You can just use the link in a browser. But adding it to your home screen gives you an app icon, opens it full screen, and means the roster still shows when you have no signal.

Android — open the link above in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu (top right), then Add to Home screen (it may say Install app). Confirm, and it appears alongside your other apps.

iPhone — open the link in Safari (it has to be Safari, not Chrome), tap the Share button at the bottom, scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.

It will not install on a work mobile at the moment. You can still open the link in the browser on a work phone and use it normally — it just will not add itself to the home screen. Use your own phone if you want the app icon.
3. Signing in — and your first password
 

When you open the app it asks you to sign in straight away — there is no separate login page. The roster, the Huddle and the circulars all show once you have signed in, and there is no other way in.

Everyone starts on their own surname — it just saves me setting a password for each person and telling you what it is. It is not meant to be a secret, which is why the next bit happens. There is a Show button by the box if you want to check what you have typed.

It will then ask you to choose your own password. That is meant to happen. Pick something only you know; you can change it later under ☰ → Settings → Password.

You then stay signed in: in the app on your home screen, for as long as you open it at least once every 60 days; in a browser, for up to 60 days. On an iPhone, sign in again once the app is on your home screen — the icon keeps its own sign-in. If it will not let you in, a “Can’t get in?” link sits under the Sign in button — tap that and it tells me to reset it for you.

4. The Pay Calculator
 

This one is built around the CEA hourly rate, so it works for you. You put in the hours you worked in a pay period and it estimates what should land in your account — tax, National Insurance, pension and London Allowance included.

It can fill most of it in from your own roster, so it is usually a case of checking rather than typing.

It is an estimate, not a payslip, and it is worth knowing that nothing you enter there leaves your phone. There is a full guide to it under the ☰ menu when you want the detail.

Anything that will not work, or a shift that looks wrong, just let me know. And if you would rather not use it at all, that is completely fine — nothing depends on it.
