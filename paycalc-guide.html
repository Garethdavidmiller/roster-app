Pay Calculator — Guide

Chiltern Railways · MYB Station · CEA & CES estimate tool

What It Does

Estimates your net take-home pay for any 4-week period. You enter the hours that earn extra; it takes off your pension, tax and National Insurance and shows the full breakdown.

It's an estimate — your payslip is the official figure that's actually paid.

Private: everything is saved on your device only. Nothing is sent to any server or seen by anyone else.

First-Time Setup

  1. Open it from the 💷 Pay button on the roster
  2. The Settings card opens — fill it in once
  3. Pick your grade (CEA or CES) — this sets your hourly rate
  4. Your hourly rate fills in automatically from your grade
  5. Enter your tax code (e.g. 1257L) — from your payslip or P60
  6. Enter your pension — the "Smart RPS CR Scheme" £ figure
  7. Pick a student loan plan if you have one, then Save settings

Choosing a Period

Use the dropdown or ← Earlier / Later → to move between 4-week periods; 📍 jumps back to now. Each period shows its payday.

Cut-off: the ✂️ marker on the roster is the Saturday before payday. Anything after it counts towards the next period.

Entering Your Hours

Your contracted hours (usually 140) are already in basic pay — only add hours that earn a higher rate or are extra:

  • Rostered Saturday — 1.25× (Saturday field)
  • Saturday on your rest day — 1.25× (use RDW, not Saturday)
  • Rest day worked — 1.25× (RDW field)
  • Overtime — 1.25× (kept separate from RDW)
  • Sunday — 1.5×
  • Bank holiday — 1.25× (row appears automatically)
  • Boxing Day (26 Dec) — 3× (row appears automatically)
Fill from calendar counts the Saturdays, Sundays, bank holidays and RDW shifts in this period and pre-fills them in one tap (filled fields turn gold). To fill just one row, tap its →. When you're online it uses your actual recorded shifts, not just the base roster.
Catching up on a whole year? The This tax year so far card has 📅 Fill these from your calendar, which does the same for every period you haven't filled in yet. It never touches a period you've already entered, and it tells you afterwards exactly which dates it filled and which it left alone — so it can't quietly overwrite a figure you typed.

Year to Date Figures

Tax adds up across the year, so a couple of figures make the estimate sharper. Open Year to Date Figures and copy two numbers from the Year to Date box on your latest payslip:

  • Taxable Pay — the Year to Date "Taxable Pay"
  • Tax Paid — the Year to Date "Tax Paid"

The card records which payslip the figures came from — it assumes your latest paid payslip, and you can correct it if you copied from an older one. They sharpen the estimate for the payslip right after that one; other payslips use the standard method, and the note on the card tells you which is in play (green when they're sharpening the payslip on screen). Update the figures each time a new payslip arrives.

This tax year so far. The card also totals up the payslips you've entered for the year — taxable pay, tax, National Insurance and take-home — plus a rough full-year projection. It's built only from the payslips you've filled in, so it gets more accurate as the year goes on.

Holiday Pay Premium (HPP)

HPP is 7.69% of your variable pay — the Saturday, RDW, overtime, Sunday, bank-holiday and Boxing Day premiums. It adds up across the tax year and is paid as a lump sum each January, for the year just ended. London Allowance is not included — it's a fixed amount paid every period (including while you're on leave), so it needs no premium.

The card builds the estimate from the payslips you've entered — it shows how many are in (for example “3 paid and 1 upcoming payslips entered”), so a small figure early in the year makes sense. You can choose where this year's estimate comes from: from your payslips in the calculator (most accurate), a rough estimate from your Year to Date pay, or enter the figure yourself.

On every payslip except that January one, HPP is shown in the HPP card only — it doesn't change your take-home. On the January payslip a green note shows the estimated premium; tick "Add this to this payslip's take-home estimate" to fold it into the gross and net. It stays off until you tick it. When your real figure lands, enter the confirmed amount in the box — it unlocks once that January payslip has been paid — to replace the estimate.

Pay Rise Back Pay

When a pay award is backdated to April, the Pay Rise Back Pay card works out the lump owed. A plain-English line at the top tells the story — which award, paid on which payslip, backdated to which April, and your rough figure. It follows the payslip you're viewing, so each tax year shows its own award; the tag beside the card title names the year. For a confirmed award the old and new rates are on record and shown as one line, and the lump lands on the right payslip automatically. For a payslip that has already been paid you can instead choose "Enter the amount from your payslip" and type the exact figure printed there — that option unlocks once the payslip exists.

Agreed but not on payslips yet? A note on the card flags that the figures are an estimate until the award is confirmed — the rest of the calculator keeps using your current rate, and your Settings rate updates by itself once the award lands.
Like HPP, the back pay isn't added to any payslip until you choose. On the payslip it's due to land, a green note appears — tick "Add this lump sum to this payslip's take-home estimate" to include it.

Data & Privacy

Everything you enter here — settings, payslip hours, Year to Date figures, Holiday Pay Premium and back pay — lives on your phone only and is never uploaded. That is deliberate: your pay is nobody else's business. The trade-off is that it doesn't follow your account, so it won't appear by itself on a new phone or at a different web address.

💾 Move Your Pay Data, the last card on the calculator, is how you carry it across: save a copy — download a file or copy it as text — then restore it on the other device. Do it before you change phone or clear your browser data. Settings has a link to the same card.

Restoring replaces, it doesn't merge. Whatever is on the device you restore onto is replaced by the copy — so restore onto the new device, not back over figures you've since entered. The app asks you to confirm first, and a backup that belongs to someone else is refused.

Pension is saved per payslip: change it mid-year and past payslips keep their own amount; the label names the payslip you're editing.

If You Opt Out of the Pension

Tick “I'm not in the pension scheme” in Settings and you're asked which payslip that started from — pick the first one with no pension deduction on it. Everything before it keeps the pension it was actually paid with, so your past estimates still match your payslips.

If you're auto-enrolled back in later — which happens roughly every three years — untick the box while looking at the first payslip that has a deduction again. The calculator keeps the whole history, so a year with a gap in the middle still adds up correctly.
