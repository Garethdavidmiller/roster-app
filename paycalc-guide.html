Pay Calculator — Guide

Chiltern Railways · MYB Station · CEA & CES estimate tool

What It Does

Estimates your net take-home pay for any 4-week period. You enter the hours that earn extra; it applies your tax code, pension and National Insurance and shows the full breakdown.

It's an estimate — your payslip is the official figure that's actually paid.

Private: everything is saved on your device only. Nothing is sent to any server or seen by anyone else.

First-Time Setup

  1. Open it from the 💷 Pay button on the roster
  2. The Settings card opens — fill it in once
  3. Pick your grade (CEA or CES) — this sets your hourly rate
  4. Check the hourly rate matches your latest payslip
  5. Enter your tax code (e.g. 1257L) — from your payslip or P60
  6. Enter your pension — the "Smart RPS CR Scheme" £ figure
  7. Pick a student loan plan if you have one, then Save settings

Choosing a Period

Use the dropdown or ← Earlier / Later → to move between 4-week periods; 📍 jumps back to now. Each period shows its payday.

Cut-off: the ✂️ marker on the roster is the Saturday before payday. Anything after it counts towards the next period.

Entering Your Hours

Your 140 contracted hours are already in basic pay — only add hours that earn a higher rate or are extra:

  • Rostered Saturday — 1.25× (Saturday field)
  • Saturday on your rest day — 1.25× (use RDW, not Saturday)
  • Rest day worked — 1.25× (RDW field)
  • Overtime — 1.25× (kept separate from RDW)
  • Sunday — 1.5×
  • Bank holiday — 1.25× (row appears automatically)
  • Boxing Day (26 Dec) — 3× (row appears automatically)
Fill from calendar counts the Saturdays, Sundays, bank holidays and RDW shifts in this period and pre-fills them in one tap (filled fields turn gold). To fill just one row, tap its →. When you're online it uses your actual recorded shifts, not just the base roster.

Holiday Pay Premium (HPP)

HPP builds up on your variable pay (Saturday, RDW, overtime, Sunday, bank-holiday and Boxing Day premiums) at 7.69% across the year, and is paid as a lump sum each January for the tax year just ended. London Allowance is not included — it's a fixed amount paid every period (including while you're on leave), so it needs no premium. The HPP card tracks your running total.

On every payslip except that January one, HPP is shown in the HPP card only — it doesn't change your take-home. On the January payslip a green note shows the estimated premium; tick "Add this to this payslip's take-home estimate" to fold it into the gross and net. It stays off until you tick it. When your real figure lands, enter the confirmed amount in the HPP card to replace the estimate.

Year to Date Figures

Tax adds up across the year, so a couple of figures make the estimate sharper. Open Year to Date Figures and copy two numbers from the Year to Date box on your latest payslip:

  • Taxable Pay — the Year to Date "Taxable Pay"
  • Tax Paid — the Year to Date "Tax Paid"

Pay Rise Back Pay

When a pay award is backdated to April, open the Pay Rise Back Pay card — it fills itself in from the current award. Your current rate is already the Old figure and the offered award % (e.g. 3.6) is pre-filled, so the New boxes and the lump sum owed across every period since April are worked out for you. Adjust any box if you know the confirmed rates, and pick which payslip it lands on.

Offered but not agreed yet? A note on the card flags that the figures are an estimate until the award is confirmed — the rest of the calculator keeps using your current, confirmed rate.
Like HPP, the back pay isn't added to any payslip until you choose. On the payslip it's due to land, a green note appears — tick "Add this lump sum to this payslip's take-home estimate" to include it. Tap "Apply new rate" once the award is confirmed so every future estimate uses the new rate.

Data & Privacy

Settings and Year to Date figures live on your phone only — never uploaded. Clear your browser data or switch phone and you'll re-enter them. Pension is saved per period: change it mid-year and past periods keep their own amount; the label shows which period you're editing.
