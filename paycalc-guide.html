Pay Calculator — Guide

Chiltern Railways · MYB Station · CEA & CES estimate tool

What It Does

Estimates your net take-home pay for any 4-week period. You enter the hours that earn extra; it takes off your pension, tax and National Insurance and shows the full breakdown.

It's an estimate — your payslip is the official figure that's actually paid.

Private: everything is saved on your device only. Nothing is sent to any server or seen by anyone else.

First-Time Setup

Open it from the 💷 Pay button on the roster. The ⚙️ Your Settings card opens first — it is a check once per tax year job, not a form you fill in from scratch.

  1. Pick your grade — CEA (Customer Experience Ambassador) or CES (Customer Experience Supervisor)
  2. Your hourly rate fills in from the grade and is locked — there is nothing to type, and it moves itself with each pay award
  3. Check your tax code — most people are 1257L; it's on your payslip or P60
  4. Check the pension contribution — already filled with the current figure. Your payslip calls it “Smart RPS CR Scheme”
  5. Set a student loan plan only if one is deducted on your payslip, then Save settings
Pension is saved per payslip. The card names which payslip you are editing. Change it mid-year and earlier payslips keep the amount they were actually paid with, so your past estimates still match.
Student loan — only if payroll deducts it. Repaying by direct debit? Leave it as None. A plan applies to every payslip once set. There's a separate tick for a Postgraduate Loan (6%), which is repaid on top of any plan.

Choosing a Period

Tabs at the top pick the tax year; below them the dropdown or ← Earlier / Later → move between 4-week periods, and 📍 jumps back to now. Each period is labelled with its payday and its payslip number — Paid 25 Sept 2026 · P28.

Cut-off: the ✂️ marker on the roster is the Saturday before payday. Anything after it counts towards the next period.

Entering Your Hours

Your contracted hours (usually 140) are already in basic pay — only add hours that earn a higher rate or are extra. The rows are grouped in three:

  • Weekend & Bank Holiday Days — Rostered Saturday · Sunday Working · Bank Holiday Rostered · Bank Holiday Overtime
  • Overtime & Rest Day Working — Overtime · Rest Day Working
  • Occasional Days — Training Days, and 🎁 Boxing Day Working in the period that has one

Each row shows its multiplier and what that is per hour for you — 1.25× · £26.86/hr — so you can sanity-check a figure without doing the sum.

Typing hours. Each row has an hrs box and a mins box. If your payslip shows decimals, just type the decimal into the hrs box — 7.5 becomes 7h 30m by itself. Everything saves as you type.
Fill from calendar counts the Saturdays, Sundays, bank holidays and RDW shifts in this period and pre-fills them in one tap (filled fields turn gold). To fill just one row, tap its →. When you're online it uses your actual recorded shifts, not just the base roster — the box says “✓ Includes recorded changes” when it has them.
Catching up on a whole year? The This tax year so far block has 📅 Fill these from your calendar, which does the same for every period you haven't filled in yet. It never touches a period you've already entered, and it tells you afterwards exactly which dates it filled and which it left alone.
Something one-off — a correction, a missing student-loan deduction, an expense? Unusual deductions or corrections at the foot of the card takes a single adjustment for this payslip only.

Full pay breakdown opens the detail — basic pay line by line, London Allowance, and each extra at its own rate. A student loan appears there when you have a plan set.

As you scroll, a slim bar keeps the payday and the take-home figure on screen, so you never lose the number you came for.

Check against your payslip. Once a payslip has been paid, a box under the result lets you type the take-home printed on it. The app compares it with the estimate and tells you how close they are — an exact match, within a pound or two (normal payroll rounding), or further off with a pointer to what usually causes it. A difference is information, never an error.
Small tags under the take-home figure show what fed this number — for example that Year to Date figures sharpened the tax, that back pay or Holiday Pay Premium was added, or that hours came from your calendar. They only appear when there's something to flag.

Year to Date Figures

Tax adds up across the year, so a couple of figures make the estimate sharper. Open 📄 Year to Date Figures and copy two numbers from the Year to Date box on your latest payslip:

  • Taxable Pay — the Year to Date "Taxable Pay"
  • Tax Paid — the Year to Date "Tax Paid"

The card asks which payslip the figures came from — it assumes your latest paid one, and you can correct it if you copied from an older one. They sharpen the estimate for the payslip right after that one; other payslips use the standard method, and the note on the card tells you which is in play. Update the figures each time a new payslip arrives.

This tax year so far. A block above the card totals the payslips you've entered — taxable pay, tax, National Insurance and take-home — plus a rough full-year projection, and it names the paydays you haven't filled in yet. It's built only from what you've entered, so it gets more accurate as the year goes on.

Sunday vs RDW — the one that catches people out. On the roster a worked Sunday is recorded as RDW, and your payslip prints it as “RDW Sun 1.5”. In the calculator those hours go in Sunday Working, not Rest Day Working — that is what applies the 1.5× instead of 1.25×. “Fill from calendar” puts them in the right box for you.
Two bank-holiday rows, not one. Rostered is the bank holiday you were down to work anyway; Overtime is the extra on top, or a rest day that landed on one. They pay the same 1.25× but appear as different lines on your payslip. Both rows only show in a period that actually contains a bank holiday.

Holiday Pay Premium (HPP)

When you take annual leave, Chiltern pay your basic rate only — you miss out on the overtime and rest-day pay you would otherwise have earned. To make up for it they calculate a Holiday Pay Premium and pay it as a lump sum each January, for the tax year just ended.

It is 7.69% of your variable pay — the Saturday, RDW, overtime, Sunday, bank-holiday and Boxing Day premiums (that's 4 weeks' leave out of 52). Basic pay, London Allowance, peer training and expenses are not included. London Allowance is left out because it is a fixed amount paid every period, including while you're on leave, so it needs no premium.

No extra entries are needed — the estimate builds itself from the hours you've entered on each payslip. You can choose where this year's figure comes from: from your payslips in the calculator (most accurate), a rough estimate from your Year to Date pay, or enter the figure yourself.

On every payslip except that January one, HPP is shown in the HPP card only — it doesn't change your take-home. On the January payslip a green note shows the estimated premium; tick “Add this to this payslip's take-home estimate” to fold it into the gross and net. It stays off until you tick it. When your real figure lands, enter the confirmed amount in the box — it unlocks once that January payslip has been paid — to replace the estimate.

Pay Rise Back Pay

When a pay award is backdated to April, the 📈 Pay Rise Back Pay card works out the lump owed. A plain-English line at the top tells the story — which award, paid on which payslip, backdated to which April, and your rough figure. It follows the payslip you're viewing, so each tax year shows its own award; the tag beside the card title names the year. For a confirmed award the old and new rates are on record and shown as one line, and the lump lands on the right payslip automatically. For a payslip that has already been paid you can instead choose “Enter the amount from your payslip” and type the exact figure printed there.

It tells you when it's short. If some of the backdated payslips have no hours saved, the card says so by name — those periods were counted at basic-rate arrears only, so any overtime or weekend arrears they carried are missing. Fill those payslips in for the full figure.
Like HPP, the back pay isn't added to any payslip until you choose. On the payslip it's due to land, a green note appears — tick “Add this lump sum to this payslip's take-home estimate” to include it.

If You Opt Out of the Pension

Tick “I'm not in the pension scheme” in Settings and you're asked which payslip that started from — pick the first one with no pension deduction on it. Everything before it keeps the pension it was actually paid with, so your past estimates still match your payslips.

If you're auto-enrolled back in later — which happens roughly every three years — untick the box while looking at the first payslip that has a deduction again. The calculator keeps the whole history, so a year with a gap in the middle still adds up correctly.

Decimal Hours Converter

Chiltern payslips print hours as decimals. The 🔢 Decimal Hours Converter card turns one into hours and minutes and back, for when you're checking a payslip line against what you actually worked. It changes nothing in the calculator — it's just there so you don't need a second app.

Data & Privacy

Everything you enter here — settings, payslip hours, Year to Date figures, Holiday Pay Premium and back pay — lives on your phone only and is never uploaded. That is deliberate: your pay is nobody else's business. The trade-off is that it doesn't follow your account, so it won't appear by itself on a new phone or at a different web address.

💾 Move Your Pay Data, the last card on the calculator, is how you carry it across: save a copy — Download backup or Copy as text — then restore it on the other device. Do it before you change phone or clear your browser data. Settings has a link to the same card.

The backup is readable text. It holds your hours, tax code, pension and Year to Date figures — and, for any payslip you've checked against the real one, your actual gross pay, tax, National Insurance and take-home. Keep it somewhere private and delete old copies.
Restoring replaces, it doesn't merge. Whatever is on the device you restore onto is replaced by the copy — so restore onto the new device, not back over figures you've since entered. The app asks you to confirm first, and a backup that belongs to someone else is refused.
