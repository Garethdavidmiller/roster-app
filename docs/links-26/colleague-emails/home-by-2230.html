Nothing has been decided. The new link is due to start in January 2027, and Sara, the RMT rep, is leading the change. This email does not say which proposal should be chosen. It shows which one fits you best, and what each would mean for you.

The closest fit for you
Middle Ground
No proposal gives you fewer closing shifts, but Middle Ground’s are the easiest to live with.
• The shortest closing shifts. Every one is 15:45–23:55, 8h 10m, Saturdays included. Today’s start half an hour earlier, at 15:15, and run 8h 40m, or 9h 10m on a Saturday.
• Kept together. They fall in just 12 weeks a year, so most of your weeks have none.
• Easy to give away. About 14 swaps a year would hand every one to a colleague.
First, the hard part
 

All three give you more closing shifts than today: about 36 a year, against 31. That is because three people will stay to the close every day, where today there are two. It adds up to about 51 hours a year at work after 22:30, against 44 today. No proposal avoids this, so choosing between them cannot bring the number down. What does differ is how long each closing shift is, how they are grouped, and how easily you can give them away. On all three you still get at least 14 hours 35 minutes off after each one, the same as today.

If 22:30 itself is too late
 

Everything above assumes a 22:30 finish is fine for you. If it is not, the answer changes: on Middle Ground and Level Crossing most lates finish at exactly 22:30.

If 22:30 is too late, Balanced V6 suits you best: 64 lates a year finish at 22:30 or later, against 82 on Middle Ground and 84 on Level Crossing (57 today). Most of its other lates are 13:30–22:00.

Giving closing shifts away
 
• Swap for a late that ends by 22:30. On all three, 4 colleagues are on a late finishing between 21:00 and 22:30 every day, Monday to Saturday, most at 22:00 or 22:30. Today there are 2 or 3.
• A whole week in one go. On every proposal, about 6 weeks a year are all closing shifts, so one swap with a colleague who likes closing hands over the whole week.
• Sundays are overtime. The Sunday closing shift, 15:15–23:25, is overtime. Mark yourself unavailable for it in advance and a colleague will take it.
What Middle Ground gives up
 
• Lots of lates finish right on your limit. 46 a year finish at exactly 22:30, against 26 today. If that is too close for comfort, see “If 22:30 itself is too late”.
• Long runs of lates. Up to 5 weeks of lates in a row, or 7 if the cover weeks either side are lates too. Today it is 2, or 3.
• Sunday pay. If you work your share of Sundays, you would earn about £350 a year less than today, before tax: the lowest of the three.
The other two
 

Level Crossing is almost the same for you: the same 12 weeks and 14 swaps. The difference is 6 Saturday closing shifts a year that start at 15:15, 8h 40m long.

Balanced V6 spreads its closing shifts more thinly, over 14 weeks a year, so more of your weeks have one, and it would take about 18 swaps to give them all away. Some start at 15:00 and run 8h 55m. In return, more of its other lates finish by 22:00.
