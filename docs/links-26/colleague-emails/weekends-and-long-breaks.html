Nothing has been decided. The new link is due to start in January 2027, and Sara, the RMT rep, is leading the change. This email does not say which proposal should be chosen. It shows which one fits you best, and what each would mean for you.

The closest fit for you
Middle Ground
• Your days off come in pairs. About 36 times a year you have two days off together, and one day of leave turns each into three.
• The most football weekends. 14 a year with no leave, never more than 5 weeks apart.
• The least leave for your trips. A football weekend and a three-day trip each month takes about 8 days of leave a year, or about 6 if you give up your Sundays. That is the fewest of the three either way.
Why it comes down to pairs
 

What matters for you is how your days off are grouped. A day of leave next to a pair of days off gives you three in a row. Next to a single day off it gives you only two, so a trip needs a second day of leave.

Why not Balanced V6
 
• It looks best at first. It has the most long breaks for free: 6 a year, and 4 of them are four days.
• But too many of its days off are single days. 22 a year, against 12 on Middle Ground. It also has 10 fewer pairs. So more of your trips need two days of leave instead of one.
• And your weekends use up its pairs. A football weekend is itself a pair. With fewer pairs to go round, every weekend you take leaves fewer for trips.
• So it needs more leave. A weekend and a three-day trip each month takes about 12 days of leave on Balanced V6, against 8 on Middle Ground. If you work your Sundays it also has 2 fewer football weekends, 12 a year.
Why not Level Crossing
 

It is close. It has the same weekends and even more pairs, 40 a year. But it has only 2 long breaks for free, against 4 on Middle Ground, so the same three-day trips take about 2 more days of leave a year.

What Middle Ground gives up
 
• Fewer free long breaks than today. 4 a year, against 13 today. Most of your trips would need a day of leave.
• Four-day trips cost more than today. About 20 days of leave a year against 18 today, or 12 against 9 with your Sundays given up. For three-day trips the two are about level.
• Sunday pay, if you work your Sundays. About £350 a year less than today before tax, the least of the three.
Making your leave go further
 
• A worked Saturday becomes a weekend. Book one day of leave on the Saturday, when your Sunday is off.
• Give up the Sunday too. If the Sunday is a rostered shift, make yourself unavailable for it in advance, and the weekend is yours. ☀ Sundays given up
• Four days cost two. On every proposal, a pair of days off needs two days of leave to become four.
• Book early. Fridays, Mondays and Saturdays are popular, and leave depends on the depot’s quota for each day.
Your leave allowance
 

You have 32 days of leave a year. On Middle Ground, a football weekend and a three-day trip each month would use about 8 of them, or about 6 with your Sundays given up. That leaves 24 to 26 days for holidays. With four-day trips it would use about 20, or 12 with your Sundays given up.

Every figure is worked out from each proposal’s weeks, the same weeks the New CEA link page shows. Each leave figure is the fewest days of leave that give that many weekends and trips, with no day used twice. It is averaged over every week of the rota you could start the year on. It assumes the leave is available when you want it, and leaves out cover weeks, the spare weeks whose four shifts are set nearer the time. Anything marked ☀ Sundays given up assumes you make yourself unavailable for the Sundays you need. A football weekend is a Saturday and Sunday off. Pounds are before tax, at £21.49 an hour.
