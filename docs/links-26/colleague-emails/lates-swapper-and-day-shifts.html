Nothing has been decided. The new link is due to start in January 2027, and Sara, the RMT rep, is leading the change. This email does not say which proposal should be chosen. It shows which one fits you best, and what each would mean for you.

The closest fit for you
Balanced V6
It is the best, or close to it, whichever way you go.
• If you stay on lates: your swaps earn the most of the three, about 24 hours’ extra pay a year. Level Crossing makes the swapping simplest, at a little less pay.
• If you take day shifts from 07:30: it keeps you the most of them, 34 a year, and your swaps still earn the most, about 28 hours a year.
• If a 07:00 start is fine: Middle Ground keeps 2 more day shifts, but Balanced V6’s swaps still earn more.
If you keep swapping onto lates
 

You would have 84 earlies a year to give away on any of the three, against 88 today. What changes is how easy each swap is, and how much it earns you.

If you also take day shifts
 

You would keep the earlies that start later, and swap only the ones that start at 06:20. Each proposal’s later earlies are different:

• Balanced V6: 07:30–16:00 and 08:00–16:30.
• Level Crossing: 07:30–16:00 and 07:15–15:45.
• Middle Ground: 07:00–15:30 and 08:00–16:30.
• Today: 08:00–16:30, which all three mostly drop.

• If a 07:00 start is fine too. Middle Ground keeps 36 day shifts, with about 24 swaps and 22 hours’ extra pay (about £600). Balanced V6 is unchanged, at 34 and about £750.
• Your weeks break up. Keeping some days of a week means the whole-week swaps mostly go. On Level Crossing that takes you from about 20 swaps a year to about 30.
• Watch the 12-hour rule. A late, then a day shift the next morning, leaves less than 12 hours off. So a 06:20 the day before a day shift has to go for another early, not a late. That happens 2 to 4 times a year on each proposal.
What Balanced V6 gives up
 
• A few more swaps if you stay on lates. About 26 a year against 20, because some of its weeks mix earlies and lates.
• Weeks that change more. Your start time moves more from one week to the next than on the other two.
• More days off on their own. 22 single rest days a year, against 10 today, and 12 full weekends off, where the other two have 14.
The other two
 

Level Crossing is the simplest if you are sure you will stay on lates: about 20 whole-week swaps a year, and nearly as much extra pay as Balanced V6. But if you take day shifts it keeps only 20 from 07:30, and your swaps rise to about 30.

Middle Ground keeps the most day shifts if a 07:00 start suits you, 36 a year. But if it does not, it keeps almost none, and its swaps earn the least of the three either way.

Every figure is counted from each proposal’s weeks, the same weeks the New link page shows, for one person over a year, Monday to Saturday, as Sundays are overtime. Cover weeks, the spare weeks whose four shifts are set nearer the time, are left out. Swaps to arrange count a whole week of earlies, or a run of 06:20s in one week, as one swap. Extra paid time assumes each early is swapped for an average late on the same day, paid at time and a quarter; it is an estimate, not a promise. The New link page’s own swap figures are for a narrower case, the shortest earlies swapped for the longest late, so they differ. An early starts before 11:00, a late at 11:00 or later. Pounds are before tax, at £21.49 an hour.
