Nothing has been decided. The new link is due to start in January 2027, and Sara, the RMT rep, is leading the change. This email does not say which proposal should be chosen. It shows which one fits you best, and what each would mean for you.

The closest fit for you
Level Crossing
• The most 06:20 starts. 58 a year, the closest to today’s 62. The other two have 50 and 48.
• More 06:20 shifts to swap onto. 5 people start at 06:20 every weekday, one more than today and than the other two.
• A week of earlies is earlies every day. No week mixes earlies and lates, and your start time changes least from week to week.
• Long runs of earlies. Up to 5 weeks of earlies in a row, against 2 today.

What Level Crossing gives up
 
• Long runs of lates too. Up to 5 weeks of lates in a row, so more of them to swap out of at once. A whole week of them could go in one swap, though (below).
• Fewer long breaks. About 2 breaks a year of three or four rest days, against 13 today. But more of your rest days come at least two together: about 42 such breaks a year, against 34 today, and 14 full weekends off, against 10.
• Fewer early finishes. 20 earlies a year finish before 14:00, against 26 today. Balanced V6 has the same.
Swapping onto earlies
 
• Swap a whole week at a time. No week on Level Crossing mixes earlies and lates, so a week of lates could go for a colleague’s week of earlies in one swap, their rest days with it. Middle Ground is the same; Balanced V6 has 3 weeks that mix, and today has 7.
• The 12-hour rule. An 06:20 start the morning after a late never gives you 12 hours off. So in a week of lates, you can only swap onto a single early the day after a rest day.
• A swap changes your hours less. On the same day, a late is about 16 minutes longer than an early on Level Crossing, against 32 today.
• Single-day swaps may be harder to arrange. Colleagues who swap their early for your late often do it for the overtime, and on all three there is less overtime in it than today.
Whichever is chosen
 
• Fewer, longer earlies. 84 a year, against 88 today, and each 7 to 10 minutes longer on average.
• More shifts to the close. About 36 lates a year run to 23:55, against 31 today, because three people stay to the close every day. Each closing shift is shorter: 8h 10m on a weekday, against 8h 40m.
The other two
 

Balanced V6 suits you better if you would rather your lates came in shorter runs: 3 weeks in a row at most. It also has the most breaks of three or four rest days. But it has fewer 06:20 starts (50), some weeks mix earlies and lates, and it has 22 single rest days a year, against 10 today.

Middle Ground suits you better if shorter shifts matter more than start times: no shift on any day is over 8h 30m. But it has the fewest 06:20 starts (48), the fewest earlies finishing before 14:00 (12 a year), and only 4 people starting at 06:20 each weekday.
