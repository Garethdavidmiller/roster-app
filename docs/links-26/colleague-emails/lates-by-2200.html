The closest fit for you
Balanced V6
• Lates that finish by 22:00. About 30 a year, nearly as many as today’s 31. The other two have 10 and 12.
• More lates to swap onto. 3 colleagues on a 22:00 late every weekday. The other two have 1.
• Sundays as good as any. The same Sunday hours as Level Crossing, more than Middle Ground.
• Longer breaks. The most breaks of three or four rest days: about 6 a year, against 2 and 4.

What Balanced V6 gives up
 
• More late finishes than today. About 64 lates a year finish after 22:00, against 57 now. That is still the fewest of the three.
• Less settled weeks. Some weeks mix earlies and lates, and your start time moves more from one week to the next.
• More days off on their own. 22 single rest days a year, against 10 today. And 12 full weekends off, where the other two have 14.
• A harder worst case. If a cover week falls badly, up to 10 days at work in a row (today 9).
Swapping onto lates
 
• More choice on weekdays. Balanced V6 has 3 colleagues each weekday on a late finishing by 22:00: 13:30–22:00 or 12:00–20:30. The other two have 1, on 11:00–19:30. Today has 1 or 2.
• Less choice on Saturdays. None on Balanced V6 or Level Crossing, and 1 on Middle Ground, against 4 today.
• The 12-hour rule still applies. A late finishing at 22:00 and an early the next morning leave less than 12 hours off. So a single swap onto a late fits the day before a rest day or another late. That is true on every link, today’s included.
Your Sundays
 
• Your share. 20 Sundays a year on all three, at time and a half. Today it is 21.
• The pay. Working your share, about £200 a year less than today on Balanced V6 and Level Crossing, and about £350 less on Middle Ground, before tax. That includes two more Saturdays at time and a quarter.
• Inside 22:00, Sundays mean earlies. No Sunday late finishes by 22:00 on any of the three; today about 3 a year do. The Sunday earlies are 07:15–15:45 and 09:00–18:00, about 10 a year.
• More Sundays going spare. 10 people work every Sunday instead of 8, so there are more shifts a colleague may want to give away.
• Your longest break. 30 days off for 14 days’ leave costs you 1 Sunday on Balanced V6, and 2 on the other two.
The other two
 

Level Crossing and Middle Ground give steadier weeks: no week mixes earlies and lates, and there are 14 full weekends off. But most of their lates finish at 22:30, about 48 and 46 a year, against 28 on Balanced V6. Middle Ground also has the fewest Sunday hours and the least Sunday pay.
