The quick check

There are only three kinds of morning rule. Work out which one you are looking at and the rest follows. Weekends and bank holidays are clear for all of them.

✓ No Railcard time limit at all
16–17 Saver · Disabled Persons
The discount works at any time. The ticket can still be restricted — an Off-Peak into Marylebone before 10:00 is invalid whatever card paid for it.
£ Minimum fare before 10:00 — not a ban
16–25 · 26–30 · HM Forces · Veterans
They may travel; the discounted fare just cannot fall below £12. Never on Advance. Waived in July & August for 16–25, HM Forces and Veterans — not for 26–30.
⊘ Fixed Railcard cutoff — same everywhere
Two Together 09:30 · Annual Gold Card 09:30 · Network 10:00
A clock time set by the Railcard itself, not by the ticket. Network also carries a £13 minimum fare Mon–Fri.
↳ Follows the ticket — no Railcard time of its own
Family & Friends · Senior · GroupSave
The discount becomes available when the equivalent Off-Peak fare does. So the answer is the ticket's rule, and at Marylebone that depends on which way they are going — see below.
· Other concession
Jobcentre Plus
50% off selected ticket types. There is no blanket morning rule to apply — check the ticket type is one the card discounts.

Out, to south of Banbury Cannot depart 04:29–08:30
Out, to Banbury & north No morning restriction
Pay-as-you-go area Aylesbury Vale Parkway, Aylesbury, Princes Risborough, High Wycombe and south Cannot depart before 09:30, or 16:00–19:00

Super Off-Peak is a different, later ticket: weekdays it cannot be used arriving Marylebone before 11:30, or departing London 04:30–08:30 or 16:00–19:00. Weekends and bank holidays are Off-Peak all day.

This is the ticket's rule, not the Railcard's, and it is a guide to the common case. Individual restriction codes, contactless and pay-as-you-go all differ — the fare and restriction shown by the retail system is the authority.

£ = the discount still applies, subject to a minimum fare ⊘ = the discount is not available yet ↳ = follows the ticket's own Off-Peak rule
Neither symbol says the passenger cannot travel. They may travel on any ticket that is valid for that train — at the undiscounted price if the discount does not apply.
Weekends & bank holidays — the morning and minimum-fare limits on this page do not apply. Who can travel, the card's area, and any companion/child rules still do.

Common gotchas

Two Together If only one named person is travelling, the discount is invalid — full fare due. Most common misuse. The rule is that both travel the whole journey together, not that both are standing at the barrier now.
Family & Friends Adults cannot use it alone — at least one child aged 5–15 must be travelling with a cardholder, for the whole journey. Under-5s exception: if the only children travelling are under 5, buy a child ticket for one of them to unlock the adult discount.
Under-5s Children under 5 travel free with a fare-paying adult — no railcard, no ticket. But on Family & Friends, where every child is under 5, buy a discounted child ticket for one of them to unlock the adult discount.
Age Don't judge validity by how old somebody looks. 16–25 and 26–30 both stay valid past their upper age; the 16–17 Saver will too from 17 August 2026; and Family & Friends can keep discounting a child who has turned 16 during the card's life. The expiry date is the check.
Minimum fare Charge the higher of the discounted fare or the minimum: £12 on 16–25, 26–30, HM Forces and Veterans; £13 on Network. Senior, Two Together, Family & Friends, Disabled Persons and 16–17 have no adult minimum. Never on Advance.
London & Oyster A Railcard discount can be set on an Oyster card, but is not available on contactless pay as you go at all. And four of these cards are excluded from the London discount entirely: 16–17 Saver, Two Together, Family & Friends and Network. Travelcards can carry their own special minimum fares — check the current fare rather than a remembered figure.
Season tickets Most railcards do not discount season tickets — Chiltern lists Season, Duo and Flexi-Season as "railcard discount not applicable". The exceptions are Jobcentre Plus and the 16–17 Saver, whose card shows a separate "Season ticket valid to date". A Gold Card is issued with an annual season — it doesn't discount one.
First Class There is no single First Class rule — each card's Class row says what it does. Network and GroupSave are Standard only; Family & Friends is Standard with a weekend upgrade option; 16–17 is Standard on ordinary tickets; the rest vary by fare type.
Digital A screenshot is not a Railcard — see Digital railcards below.

How to check one

1
Check the ticket first. Right journey, date, route, class — and is it valid on this train? An Off-Peak into Marylebone before 10:00 is invalid no matter which Railcard bought it.
2
Which discount does the ticket claim? The ticket names the Railcard it was priced with. That is the one to check — not whichever card they happen to produce.
3
Check that Railcard. Right product, in date, and shown properly — a live digital card in its own app or account, or a valid physical card (signed, where the card requires it) with any separate Photocard it needs.
4
Check the required people are travelling. Two Together — both named holders, whole journey. Family & Friends — a cardholder and a child 5–15. Network, Gold, Veterans, Disabled — the discounted group travels with the holder.
5
Then the morning rule — and use the right kind. Fixed cutoff, minimum fare, or follows-the-ticket. The quick check at the top sorts them.
6
Still unsure? Check it in the retail system before refusing. Refusing a valid discounted ticket is the worse mistake, and it is the one this page could cause.

If something is wrong

Before travel — selling or at the barrier outbound

The goal is a valid ticket before they travel. Check the Railcard, the fare available for that train, the time restriction, any minimum fare, the route, the group and the class. If the discount doesn't apply, sell what does.

After travel — inbound at the gateline

The journey has happened, so the question is whether the discounted ticket was valid, and then current Chiltern revenue procedure — excess or Penalty Fare. That is a Chiltern decision, not one this page makes.

Forgotten Railcard No valid Railcard shown means the discounted ticket cannot simply be treated as valid — follow current Chiltern excess/Penalty Fare procedure. A customer who genuinely held a valid Railcard may reclaim the extra charge: Chiltern's Passenger Charter allows one claim per customer in a 12-month period. There is no 28-day deadline — this guide used to say there was, and no source supports it.

Digital railcards

A digital Railcard is held in the official Railcard app or in the account of the retailer it was bought from — Trainline and others are legitimate. Ask to see the live card in that app or account.

What you are checking is the same in every app: the right product, in date, the photo and name, and the right people travelling. A static screenshot is not a Railcard — the card has to be displayed live.

Don't rely on one app's animation as a test of authenticity. Retailer displays differ and change; the product, the dates and the photo do not.

When selling a discounted ticket, ask to see the Railcard on screen before pricing the journey. If the phone is dead or the app won't open, treat it as no Railcard shown — full fare outbound, Chiltern procedure inbound.
