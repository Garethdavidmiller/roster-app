List the shifts you need and how many people on each — the station is staffed in waves through the day (opens, mid-morning, middles, afternoons, closes), so add a row for every distinct start time. Weekday, Saturday, and Sunday targets are separate because the roster genuinely differs on those days. The generator keeps each line inside one wave, so a person's week stays on much the same sort of shift; the Design checks below say what the result actually came to.

A new design starts from a table built against the December 2026 train service:

  • Four turns on at the open every day, three through to the close — four on a Saturday.
  • Five still on at 22:00; fourteen working a Saturday, leaning late for events; ten on a Sunday.
  • Mon–Sat averaging exactly the contracted week.
  • Late turns are deliberately shorter than early ones, bar the one short early.
  • Every start and finish is a round time, on the quarter hour wherever the station's own opening and closing hours allow.

Monday to Saturday run mostly the same turns — Saturday's morning body starts later, and one of its lates is spent on a fourth closer. It is a starting point to argue with, not a proposal: change anything you like, and read the Design checks and heat map below before anyone takes a printout into a room.

Line order — what to tune for

These change the order of the lines, never the cover. Turning one on takes freedom from the others; the result says what each cost. Cover weeks are always kept evenly spaced around the rotation — that one is not optional, because two cover weeks side by side run into one long stretch.

Each day's total can't exceed 24 — the remaining lines become rest days.
